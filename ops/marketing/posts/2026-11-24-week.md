## Before filming (read once)

- **Format:** vertical 9:16, at least 720p. Put the main point on screen in the first 3 seconds. TikTok's own guidance says to introduce the core message within 3 seconds, keep the hook in the first 6, use captions or text overlays, and keep a "DIY or not overly polished style" ([TikTok Ads help, checked 2026-11-24](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en)). Keep each video under 60 s. YouTube blocks a Short longer than one minute everywhere if it has any copyright claim ([YouTube help, checked 2026-11-24](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)). Use platform-library or royalty-free audio.
- **Real numbers only.** Every weight, RPE, ghost result and suggestion on screen must come from a real set that day. If the app suggests something unexpected, post that. Don't stage outcomes.
- **Gym etiquette:** check the gym's filming rules, keep other members out of frame or ask first, and don't hog equipment while filming.
- **Where people can get it:** Lift Log is not in Google Play or the App Store yet (blocked on developer accounts, see `ops/roadmap.md`). The honest call to action for now is the web version (works on iPhone and Android, add to home screen) through a link Nheil puts in his bio. Don't say "download on the App Store".
- **Screen recordings:** turn on Do Not Disturb, use dark mode (it fits the brand), and zoom the edit to the card being discussed. Text on screen must be readable at phone size.
- **Bodyweight content:** Nheil's own numbers (about 180 lb now, aiming for about 165 by June, cut starting mid-February) are his to share or not. Every script below works with or without the exact numbers.

## 1. "Cutting without losing your bench": the pace math (cut, lifting value)

- **Feature shown:** Bodyweight and cut tracker: Trend, weekly Rate with colour, goal date and the pace it needs (**coming soon, PR #1 not merged**. Don't post the app part until it is in the build Nheil uses.)
- **Hook (0–2 s):** Nheil holding a plate, straight to camera: "I'm cutting 15 pounds and I refuse to lose my bench."
  - Variation without his numbers: "Most people cut too fast. Here's how to pick your pace."
- **Shot list:**
  1. (0–2 s) Hook to camera between sets, bench in the background.
  2. (2–8 s) Close-up of phone: Progress tab, typing the goal (165 lb, Jun 1). The goal line shows the weekly pace it needs.
  3. (8–15 s) Talking head: "Research on lifters cutting points to about half to one percent of bodyweight a week if you want to keep strength. Slower keeps more."
  4. (15–22 s) Screen: the 8-week chart. Faint dots are daily weigh-ins, the solid line is the trend. "The daily scale jumps around. I only care about the line."
  5. (22–28 s) Screen: the Rate tile turning green. "Green means I'm in that range."
  6. (28–35 s) Cut to a working set of bench. End text: "I'll post the trend every week. Let's see if the bench holds."
- **On-screen text:**
  - "Cut pace: 0.5–1% of bodyweight per week"
  - "Trust the trend, not today's scale"
  - "Green = keeping strength range"
  - Small text, last 3 s: "Training guidance, not medical or diet advice"
- **Pace math for the voice-over (check against the real app before posting):** 180 → 165 lb from Feb 15 to Jun 1 is 15 lb in about 15 weeks, roughly 1 lb a week, about 0.55% of 180 lb. That sits inside the 0.5–1% range from [Helms, Aragon & Fitschen 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033492). [Garthe et al. 2011](https://www.pubmed.gov/21558571/) suggests around 0.7%/wk for athletes who want to keep gaining strength. Say "research suggests" and do not mention food, calories or macros.
- **Caption:** Starting my cut in February, logging weight now. The plan: about 1 lb a week, keep the bench. Lift Log turns daily weigh-ins into a trend and tells me if my pace is too fast. I'll post the line every week. Training guidance, not diet advice.
- **Hashtags:** #cutting #strengthtraining #benchpress #gymtok #liftingtips #fitnessjourney #collegelifter
- **Don't:** show a shirtless before shot, give calorie numbers, or call any pace "safe for everyone".

## 2. "Racing last week's me" (ghost race, app moment)

- **Feature shown:** Ghost race. Each set shows last week's numbers under it and turns green on a win or red on a loss, with a running race score.
- **Hook (0–2 s):** Split screen: Nheil under the bar, phone showing the ghost line. Text: "Me vs. last week's me."
- **Shot list:**
  1. (0–2 s) Hook split screen.
  2. (2–6 s) Phone close-up: the exercise card with the ghost line showing last week's set ("last wk: 185 × 6", whatever the real log says).
  3. (6–14 s) The set, filmed from the side, sped up 1.5×.
  4. (14–18 s) Back to the phone: log reps, the line turns green (or red).
  5. (18–30 s) Quick cuts of sets 2 and 3, the phone after each, the race score updating.
  6. (30–35 s) Final score to camera: "3–1 me. Next week he's got new numbers to beat."
- **On-screen text:**
  - "Set 1: beat it" / "Set 2: tied" / "Set 3: lost" (whatever happened)
  - "Final: you vs. last week"
  - "Progressive overload, but make it a race"
- **If you lose the race:** post it anyway. "Lost to last week's me. Slept 5 hours. Rematch Thursday." Honest losses are good content and true to the brand.
- **Caption:** Every set in Lift Log races what I did last week. Green means I beat it, red means past me won. Simple progressive overload with a scoreboard. Who wins next week?
- **Hashtags:** #progressiveoverload #gymtok #pushpulllegs #liftingmotivation #gymprogress #workouttracker

## 3. "My app picks my next weight" (RPE suggestion, app moment plus lifting value)

- **Feature shown:** RPE-based weight suggestion. Log the set with an RPE and the app suggests the next set's weight.
- **Hook (0–2 s):** Nheil racked, breathing hard, typing on phone. Text: "I told my app that set was a 9. Watch what it does."
- **Shot list:**
  1. (0–2 s) Hook: post-set, phone in hand.
  2. (2–6 s) Screen: entering reps and RPE 9 (or whatever the real RPE was).
  3. (6–10 s) Screen: the suggestion box updates with the next set's weight. Zoom on the number.
  4. (10–18 s) Talking head, quick: "RPE is how hard the set felt. 10 means nothing left, 8 means about 2 reps left. Lift Log uses it to pick my next weight up or down."
  5. (18–26 s) The next set at the suggested weight.
  6. (26–30 s) Log that set. Text: "That's coaching, not just logging."
- **On-screen text:**
  - "RPE 10 = nothing left"
  - "RPE 8 = about 2 reps left"
  - "Set felt harder → lighter next set. Easier → heavier."
  - "Lift Log: coaches every set"
- **Note:** say "about 2 reps left". RPE ratings are estimates, and the app's beginner RPE explainer isn't built yet (roadmap: Now).
- **Caption:** Most trackers just record your sets. Lift Log reads how hard the set felt (RPE) and suggests the weight for the next one. Felt like a 9? It adjusts. Felt like a 6? Go up. Built it for my own training.
- **Hashtags:** #rpe #autoregulation #strengthtraining #gymtok #liftingtips #powerbuilding #workoutapp
